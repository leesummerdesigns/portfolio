AI Expert, AI Strategy Consultant or AI Business Specialist: Who Do You Need?

Only one of these titles comes with an exam. Choose by where your AI work stopped moving.

Louis Henrich · 5 min read

← Back to Blog

Anyone in Switzerland can call themselves an AI expert, and I do. Anyone can call themselves an AI strategy consultant too. Of the three titles companies search for, only one comes with a federal exam: AI Business Specialist.

So a title tells you little about who can help you. I think you should choose by where your AI work is stuck, because each of these people is useful at a different point.

I call that place your stuck point: the last spot where your AI work stopped moving. Once you can name it, the hiring decision gets much smaller.

Each title usually means a different job

TitleWhat the person usually doesRules on the titleCall them when
AI expertKnows what AI can do and applies it. The scope varies a lot from person to person.NoneYou have a specific use case and need someone who has built something similar.
AI strategy consultantWorks out where AI is worth using and turns that into a prioritised roadmap.NoneYou don't know where to start, or your leadership team disagrees on priorities.
AI Business SpecialistConnects management, business teams and technology, plans the introduction and supports the projects.Protected with the federal diplomaYou have ideas and technical people, but nobody owns the space between them.

Only one of these titles has an exam behind it

Swiss authorities approved the exam regulations for the AI Business Specialist in October 2025, with the first exam scheduled for autumn 2026. The protected German title is AI Business Specialist mit eidgenössischem Fachausweis.

I should declare my position. I'm completing this program at IFA in Zurich and finish in 2027, so I don't hold the title yet. I'd read the diploma as evidence of training. Whether someone has shipped anything is a separate question, and you'll need to ask it yourself.

Your stuck point tells you who to call

"We don't know where to start." You need strategy work. Deloitte reports difficulty identifying use cases as a top barrier to AI returns in Switzerland, named by 27% of respondents. A good AI strategy consultant leaves you with a short list your leadership can decide on.

"We know what we want, and nobody can build it." You need an AI expert who has built things. The same Deloitte research puts lack of technical talent and skills first, at 30%. Ask to see live work.

"The pilot worked, and nothing changed." You need someone who connects the business to the technology and stays until people use the result. That's the core of the AI Business Specialist role, and it overlaps with product leadership.

"Our people are unsure about all of this." You need enablement or coaching. UBS found that many Swiss SMEs still lack resources, expertise and confidence in the benefits. A tool rollout won't fix the confidence part, which is why I also offer 1:1 coaching for leaders.

Product experience pays off after the demo

I came to AI from over 20 years in product design and leadership, so I'm biased here. A demo can show you that a model works. Only live users can show you whether anyone wants the result.

When I worked on the listing description generator at Swiss Marketplace Group, the useful number was how many people kept the generated text in their final ad. It was 90%. A demo could never have given us that figure.

If your stuck point sits after the demo, ask candidates how they measured adoption on their last project. Someone with product leadership experience should answer in numbers.

These roles overlap, and sometimes you need a bigger firm

A map like this is tidier than real life. In a small company, one person can cover strategy and delivery. I do both, and plenty of people with the same titles do something else entirely.

A large regulated company rolling out AI across several countries may need more people than one independent consultant can provide. Firms such as Deloitte hire AI strategy consultants in Zurich for this kind of work, and that can be the right call.

The test stays the same at either size: can they move your work past the place it stopped?

Ask these questions before you hire anyone

  1. What have you shipped that real users use today?
  2. Can I see it, or speak to someone who uses it?
  3. Who does the work: you or a team I haven't met?
  4. What will I be able to decide or do when you leave?
  5. Which part of our plan would you advise us to drop?

The last question tells me the most, because a person who only sells hours has little reason to shrink the job.

This week, sit down with your team and finish one sentence: "Our AI work stopped when…". You'll know who to call once you've written it. If the sentence surprises you, send it to me and I'll tell you who I'd call.
