What AI Consulting Costs in Switzerland

Published prices, from audits to day rates, and the number I would compare instead.

Louis Henrich · 5 min read

← Back to Blog

I recently asked an AI tool to name AI experts with product leadership experience. Other names came up, mine didn't, and several of those people listed their daily rates.

So I collected what Swiss AI consultants publish. Day rates are easy to find, and they tell you less than you'd expect.

The number I'd compare is the decision price: everything you pay until your company can make one specific decision or ship one specific thing. In Lady Windermere's Fan, Oscar Wilde has Lord Darlington define a cynic as someone who knows the price of everything and the value of nothing. Buying consulting by day rate carries a similar risk.

A day rate hides the number you care about

Imagine two quotes, with figures I've invented to show the arithmetic. Consultant A charges CHF 3,000 a day and needs four days. Consultant B charges CHF 2,000 a day and needs ten.

A costs CHF 12,000. B costs CHF 20,000. The higher day rate is CHF 8,000 cheaper, provided both deliver the same result.

That last condition is where you need to look. Ask each of them what you'll be able to decide or do at the end, then compare those answers along with the totals.

Three things move the decision price

How clear the question is. "Help us with AI" takes longer to price and longer to finish than "tell us which two use cases to fund next year". You can shrink a quote before anyone starts by writing the sharper version.

Who does the work. Ask whether the person you met will do it. A senior person may finish faster, while a mixed team may suit a large rollout. Either can make sense if you know which one you're buying.

Whether anything gets built. A roadmap and a live feature are different purchases. Published prices above run from an audit to integration projects, and one vendor estimates CHF 2,000 to 50,000 for introducing AI at a Swiss SME, depending on scope. Treat that range as a seller's estimate.

Published day rates still earn their place

A public rate saves you a call. It helps you rule people out quickly, and it works well for a like-for-like purchase such as a one-day workshop.

I don't publish mine. I quote per project after a short intro call, which means you can't compare me at a glance, and that's a real cost on your side. So hold me to the same standard as anyone else: expect the scope and total price in writing before you commit.

Ask for one page before you compare quotes

  1. The decision or deliverable you'll have at the end.
  2. The names of the people doing the work.
  3. The number of days, and what would change that number.
  4. The total price, including travel and VAT.
  5. What you own and can keep using after the engagement.

Those five lines make two very different proposals comparable, whatever each consultant charges per day.

Before you ask anyone for a quote, write down the one decision you want to be able to make.
